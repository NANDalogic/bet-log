Market Making 101

You can buy at the price on the shelf, or name your own price and wait. Market makers are the ones who wait.

Taker = shopper62¢ nowBuys whatever is posted. Gets in for sure. Pays the markup and the full fee.
Maker = shopkeeper58¢ maybePosts a price and waits. Better price, a quarter of the fee (or none). Might never get filled.
Think: airport currency booth

The booth buys euros at $1.05 and sells them at $1.15. It doesn't care where the euro goes next. It earns the 10¢ gap on every customer in a hurry. A Kalshi market maker does the same with YES and NO.

The one rule: YES + NO = $1

Every contract pays $1 if it happens and $0 if it doesn't. One person holds YES, another holds NO, and together they always collect exactly $1. So a price is a chance: 62¢ ≈ 62%.

62¢ + 39¢ = $1.01. That extra 1¢ is the spread: the gap between the best buy price and the best sell price. It's the shopkeeper's markup. If someone buys a YES at 62¢ from a maker and someone else sells a YES at 61¢ to that maker, the maker keeps 1¢.

The whole game in two lines

When your order fills, you beat the taker by the same $5.22 every time (4¢ better price + $1.22 less fee).

But fills come most often when news goes against you, and good news leaves you behind. Making money as a maker means the discount has to outrun those bad fills.

Before real money: the checklist

  • Know the fair price first. From bet-buddy's no-vig line. Post YES below fair, never above.
  • Tick "Submit as resting order only". Then you can never accidentally pay the ask.
  • Set Expiration before kickoff. If a key player is questionable, before inactives (90 minutes before kickoff).
  • Size for the bad fill. Injury news can move a price 10–15¢. 100 contracts × 13¢ = $13. Pick a size where that's fine.
  • Cancel before news drops. NFL injury reports come out Wednesday to Friday afternoons. Open orders are under Portfolio.
  • A fill seconds after news is a warning, not luck. Look up why before you add more.
  • Start with a hedge you'd place anyway. Worst case, it's the same position at a better price.

Quick check

Say it in 30 seconds

"On Kalshi you either buy at the posted price or post your own and wait. Waiting gets you a better price and a smaller fee. The catch: the people quickest to trade with you are often the ones who just heard news you haven't. Market makers post on both sides and keep the small gap in between, like the currency booth at the airport."
